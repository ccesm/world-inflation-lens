# Phase C0 — AI infrastructure and macroeconomic inflation transmission

Research cutoff: **2026-10-09**. Baseline: **596e7e69560ee17be0b5412e852e74032533f49a**. Independent research branch; no production change.

## Executive summary

AI infrastructure can add near-term demand for electricity, equipment, construction and financing, while successful deployment can expand productive capacity later. Supply construction, efficiency, global saving, policy, adoption delays, margins and rebound use can offset either direction. The sign, scale and persistence of the US inflation effect are **not identified** here. A long-run 5–20-year horizon is a research horizon, not a forecast sample or an assumed AI growth contribution.

C0 delivers 17 conditional hypotheses across four workstreams, a source/metadata feasibility inventory, separately graded evidence, inherited gaps, a transmission map and one gated C1 plan. All complete AI transmission hypotheses remain E0. Primary definitions and reported observations are E1; reproduced corporate cash-budget relationships are narrowly E2. No new E3 association, E4 causal estimate or numerical inflation forecast is produced.

The recommended first pilot is **regional data-center energization → electricity demand → electricity price pressure**. Observable physical timing is potentially better than an assumed national AI-investment shock, but actual energization, workload share, geography, weather/fuel controls and comparable outcome histories still require qualification. C1 has not started. If AI share is missing, any later permitted analysis must be labeled data-center-associated, never AI-attributable.

## Horizon framework

| Horizon | Potential upward forces | Potential downward / offsetting forces | What remains uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short: 0–3 years | Actual load peaks, grid/equipment bottlenecks, construction demand, financing demand | Slack, cash generation, imports, displaced investment, policy response | Actual AI load and local pass-through, not headline announced capacity |
| Medium: 3–7 years | Slow interconnection/supply, sustained demand and rebound | Completed generation/transmission, organizational adoption, lower unit costs | Implementation costs, financing supply and competition |
| Long: 7–20 years | Repeated resource constraints or demand growth | Innovation, capital deepening, aggregate supply expansion | No measured no-AI counterfactual, persistent inflation coefficient or assured annual GDP gain |

Time horizons are competing mechanisms, not additive point estimates or scenarios presented as observations.

## Workstream 1 — energy and infrastructure

**Mechanism:** energized compute/cooling increases regional electricity demand; constrained short-run supply may raise congestion or input prices. Construction and equipment demand can tighten resource markets. Actual generation/grid additions, efficiency, imports, delayed commissioning and rebound change the medium/long outcome. Hypotheses E1–E4 keep these mechanisms separate.

**Best-supported source observations:** EIA-861M sales/revenue and EIA-923 generation have official catalogs; EIA-860 distinguishes proposed from operable generators, with operable not necessarily dispatching. LBNL reports modeled historical data-center consumption and forward scenarios—not AI-only metered loads. Census separately defines data-center buildings within office construction and excludes racks/servers and land. PJM's official forecast supplement explicitly removes embedded data-center load before adding large-load adjustments, illustrating an operational double-count problem. PJM also lowered its near-term forecast after stricter request vetting and economic/EV updates; that is a forecast revision, not falling realized AI load. See EV-EIA, EV-GENERATOR, EV-CONSTRUCTION, EV-LBNL, EV-PJM and EV-PJM-REVISION.

**Counterevidence and confounders:** efficiency/demand rebound work in opposing directions; proposed requests may not energize, supply may expand, and tariff regulation may delay or redistribute costs. These are mostly countermechanisms/measurement limits, not independently replicated negative estimates. Gas prices, weather, generation outages/mix, non-AI electrification, population/industrial growth and rate cases can drive demand/prices without AI. The registry does not claim qualified transformer/GPU price or NOAA weather series yet.

**Feasibility:** a regional descriptive pilot is plausible after source gates. AI causal identification is unavailable now. EIA average revenue/kWh is not CPI electricity, a wholesale LMP or a fixed tariff. Announced campus MW is not realized electricity consumption, and all data centers are not all AI.

## Workstream 2 — financing and capital markets

**Mechanism:** large investment competes for internally generated cash; corporate debt/equity, explicit customer financing and alternative structures can fund combined spending. Sector credit conditions may mediate financing; global saving, capital inflows, cash-rich firms and policy can offset aggregate rate pressure. F1–F4 distinguish company financing, sector credit and economy-wide conditions.

**Best-supported observations:** locked Oracle/Amazon cash bridges are reproducible. H.15/FRED separates nominal and TIPS Treasury yields; Z.1 records sector financial accounts and Treasury TIC foreign flows. These do not identify an AI Treasury yield shock. FRED's corporate OAS series is originally **ICE**, a private licensed provider; its stated three-year history limitation from April 2026 makes a long credit pilot unqualified. TIC's Form S/SLT transition also requires a historical break audit. See the two funding evidence entries, EV-B2-GAPS, EV-YIELDS, EV-ICE and EV-TIC.

**Counterevidence and confounders:** cash generation/liquidity coexist with debt issuance. Borrowing can refinance maturities or fund acquisitions/distributions; negative FCF is not distress. Customer cash substitutes for some financing but has performance/refund obligations. Policy, fiscal deficits/Treasury supply, risk appetite, inflation expectations and global savings can dominate yields/spreads. No causal ranking of firms or extrapolation from Oracle/Amazon to all five firms.

**Feasibility:** extend native company bridges separately, then qualify issuer-level credit data. Aggregate real-rate analysis remains descriptive; TIPS yield is not an observed neutral rate, and official term-premium estimates are model-dependent. No synthetic debt/lease/CapEx aggregate or hidden leverage metric.

## Workstream 3 — adoption, productivity and growth

**Mechanism:** deployed AI can improve some task output, complementary capital and organization may improve firm/industry output, and lower unit costs can increase supply. Consumers receive price benefits only if costs pass through; market structure, wages, demand, quality, implementation expenses and rebound may offset them. P1–P4 separate each link.

**Best-supported observations:** Census changed BTOS AI wording in collection beginning 2025-11-17 and created a separate released series from 2025-12-04. Old goods/services use is not comparable to broader business-functions use. BLS productivity/ULC metadata and calculation identities are available. The primary authors of *Generative AI at Work* report improved customer-support task productivity; the primary author-submitted arXiv v2 abstract is qualified here, not a replicated effect or full causal-method audit. The Productivity J-Curve model supplies complementary-investment reasoning, not an observed future GDP gain. See EV-BTOS-BREAK, EV-ULC, EV-PRODUCTIVITY-PAPER and EV-JCURVE.

**Counterevidence and confounders:** some deployments improve promptly, but heterogeneity/selection/quality and adjustment costs prevent universal extrapolation. The J-curve is a possible explanation, not a required empirical pattern. Compensation, market concentration and stronger demand can absorb cost savings. Survey experimentation/planned use, actual firm deployment, task gains, industry gains, economy-wide productivity and consumer-price reductions are different observations.

**Feasibility:** the new comparable BTOS regime is short; industry concordances, weights and full replication studies still require qualification. C0 gives no annual AI GDP contribution and no causal productivity-to-inflation coefficient.

## Workstream 4 — inflation transmission

**Mechanism:** local scarcity can raise relative prices; broader demand may strain capacity, while productivity may relax it. Policy responses, expectations, imports and crowding out alter the aggregate effect. I1–I5 distinguish price outcomes and detectability.

**Best-supported observations:** CPI comes from BLS; PCE/GDP from BEA; FRED distributes these and BLS output/hour/ULC. St. Louis Fed breakeven is a Treasury-derived yield spread; SCE/SPF are surveys. A few manually verified source-page latest values are unit/date-stamped spot checks, not a time-series analysis. No numerical effect is estimated from them. See EV-INFLATION-MEASURES and EV-POLICY.

**Counterevidence and confounders:** a one-time electricity relative-price step need not produce permanently higher inflation. A productivity gain need not reduce consumer prices if margins, wages or income/demand rise. Monetary policy can stabilize aggregate inflation; local shocks can be too small or offset to detect nationally. Breakeven compensation contains risk/liquidity effects and is not pure expected inflation. These remain hypotheses or measurement distinctions, not demonstrated national AI effects.

**Feasibility:** long inflation histories cannot solve the short/poorly measured AI exposure or endogenous policy problem. Do not sum channel estimates whose underlying investment, energy or cost shocks overlap. Sector-price effects, price-level shifts, inflation rates and persistence need distinct estimands. No defensible aggregate AI causal counterfactual is qualified in C0.

## Source quality and reproducibility

The registry explicitly marks verified manual metadata versus candidates and insufficient documentation. Full numerical histories, release lags, revision policies and exact item codes remain null wherever not authenticated. None is claimed as a verified automated retrieval route. Failed source readers are recorded; primary alternatives are used narrowly, without bypassing access controls or using media figures. The NBER full-text and published-version access gaps remain visible rather than silently promoting abstracts to causal evidence.

`baseline-lock.json` preserves all inherited tracked bytes. Evidence extract generation is reproducible from locked inputs. `content-identity.json` freezes the C0 documents/registries and validation code; `validation-results.json` records actual executed checks. Neither technical success nor a source's prestige proves an economic mechanism.
