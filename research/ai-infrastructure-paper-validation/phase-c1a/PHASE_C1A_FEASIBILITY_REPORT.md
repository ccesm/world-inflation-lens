## Identification, controls and counterevidence

Weather and benchmark gas histories are available at state/common benchmark level. Matched local fuel, complete tariff/rate-case histories, historical supply/grid constraints, non-data-center industrial demand, broader electrification and regional economic controls are incomplete. Generation additions and tariffs can be mediators rather than pre-existing confounders; models conditioning on them would answer different questions.

Site selection follows power cost, reliability, local growth and incentives. These factors also drive demand and price, creating selection and reverse causality. Multiple construction/commissioning phases undermine a single binary treatment. Shared grid costs, markets and future development contaminate controls. Retail choice and customer composition affect revenue/kWh; a price change need not be a tariff increase. COVID-era demand, weather/fuel shocks and industrial openings can dominate changes. Renewable procurement, capacity expansion, efficiency and commissioning delays are countermechanisms, not measured evidence of an AI disinflation effect here.

Missing AI electricity shares prohibit AI-specific attribution. More precise general data-center exposure could later support a scoped descriptive analysis, but only after the existing protocol and gates are reviewed. No national inflation estimate, cross-channel addition or forecasting engine is justified.
