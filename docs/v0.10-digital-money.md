## Methodology and limitations

Stablecoin issuance is not equivalent to Federal Reserve money creation. Market capitalization is not a payment volume or real-economy spending measure. USD denomination does not establish holder geography or incremental dollarization. Higher crypto prices cannot identify a cause or establish dollar collapse.

Deposit effects depend on settlement and reserve placement: an issuer can hold a bank deposit, buy a bill from a seller who receives a deposit, or settle funds outside commercial banks. Aggregate deposits need not fall one-for-one with issuance. See [Federal Reserve research on banks and stablecoins](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/banks-in-the-age-of-stablecoins-lessons-from-their-historical-responses-to-financial-innovations-20260501.html). The page also cites [Federal Reserve monetary-aggregate definitions](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/new-forms-of-money-and-the-u-s-monetary-aggregates-20260904.html).

Treasury demand can involve replacement of an existing intermediary rather than net new demand. Direct bills, repo claims and money-fund shares cannot be added indiscriminately without risking double counting. Global stablecoin publication estimates and U.S. bank deposits differ in scope and date, so the site does not chart a ratio or infer deposit losses from their difference.

Still planned: licensed continuous stablecoin history; issuer-by-issuer reconciled Treasury holdings in dollars; measured deposit migration; geographic ownership/use; Bitcoin and gold price histories. No empty market charts or fabricated observations stand in for these datasets. There are no token recommendations, trading signals, price targets, collapse probabilities or AI-generated forecasts.

## Independent ingestion and refresh

- `data/productivity/` remains separate from `data/digital-money/`; the new module has its own adapter, import command, utilities and validation tests.
- `scripts/import-digital-money.mjs --input-dir <reviewed-inputs> --output-dir <staging-directory>` stages publication and bank snapshots. It does not replace production snapshots directly.
- The bank series joins the existing Monday 14:23 UTC check. The monthly source frequency is retained; weekly checks do not fabricate observations. Source changes join the existing revision ledger and atomic rollback transaction.
- Publication estimates require manual source review and an explicit Git change. Weekly refresh validates their retained snapshots but does not claim to have re-fetched or updated their publications.
- Data Health adds four research-publication entries and one bank-series entry. It distinguishes observation, publication, retrieval and manual-review dates. Bank coverage, missing counts and ledger revision counts are visible.
- Initial bank import establishes a baseline: zero missing records in this snapshot, and no prior imported vintage from which to infer historical revisions. Subsequent revisions are tracked; initial import is not evidence that the source has never revised data. The last 30 successful checks retain revision summaries; Git preserves complete changes.
- No runtime browser API calls, frontend keys, new dependencies or new workflow permissions were introduced. Pushes to main still deploy `dist` with Vite base `/world-inflation-lens/`.
